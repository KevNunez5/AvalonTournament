
We define two strategies: 
    - GoodStrategy, for the good players, and 
    - EvilStrategy, for the evil players.

Each strategy contains two main components:
    - determining a team to propose.
    - determining whether to vote in favor, or against a proposed team.
    
In addition, the EvilStrategy contains the following two components.   
    - determining whether to accept or reject a quest.
    - guessing who is Merlin.



*** GoodStrategy: ***

The idea of the GoodStrategy is that it keeps a list of 'hypotheses'. Each hypothesis is a possible assignment of roles to players.
Initially, the player will have one hypothesis for each such possible assignment.
However, as the game progresses, he will discard those hypotheses that are inconsistent with the events in the game.

Each good player should not only keep hypotheses for himself, but also, for every other player, a list of hypotheses from the point of view of that player.
This will allow the good player to detect if any player is taking an action that is inconsistent with his hypotheses, which would suggest that he is an evil player.



*** EvilStrategy: ***

The idea of the EvilStrategy is that, in general, it will do exactly the same as the GoodStrategy, because otherwise the other payers could recognize
that the player is evil. Except:
    - if a certain action leads to a direct victory for the evil players, then he will of course choose that action.
    - if a certain action is needed to prevent a victory for the good players, then he will also choose that action.
    - if the majority of 'good' players already know that he is an evil player.

Furthermore, if the GoodStrategy is indifferent between several choices, then the evil player will choose the one that is most beneficial for him.
